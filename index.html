Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) & Statutory Insights

Can a foreigner own 100% of a company in Malaysia?

Yes. Under the Malaysian Companies Act 2016, foreign nationals and international corporations can own 100% equity in a Private Limited Company (Sendirian Berhad - Sdn Bhd) across most non-restricted sectors including IT, software, engineering, management consultancy, international trading, and business advisory.

How long does it take to incorporate a Sdn Bhd with SSM?

Following name approval by Suruhanjaya Syarikat Malaysia (SSM), complete electronic incorporation is usually finalized within 3 to 5 business days, provided all identity documentation and Section 201 director consents are properly verified.

What is the minimum paid-up capital required for an ESD Employment Pass?

For a 100% foreign-owned Malaysian company sponsoring foreign personnel or directors for an Employment Pass (EP) via the Expatriate Services Division (ESD), the statutory paid-up capital is strictly RM1,000,000. Companies with 51%+ Malaysian equity require RM350,000 to RM500,000.

What is Malaysia's new Expatriate Salary Policy for June 2026?

Effective 1 June 2026, the Ministry of Home Affairs (KDN) has instituted revised minimum monthly salary benchmarks for Employment Passes (EP Category I at RM10,000+, Category II at RM5,000-RM9,999, and Category III at RM3,000-RM4,999) along with updated pass duration and talent assessment frameworks.

Is appointing a licensed Company Secretary mandatory in Malaysia?

Yes. Section 235 of the Companies Act 2016 legally requires every Malaysian Sdn Bhd to appoint at least one qualified, licensed Company Secretary who is a natural person and ordinarily resides in Malaysia within 30 days of company formation.

What is required for LHDN MyInvois e-Invoicing in 2026?

All Malaysian registered businesses must transmit and validate B2B, B2C, and B2G transaction invoices with the Inland Revenue Board of Malaysia (LHDN) either through the MyInvois Web Portal or direct ERP API integration in structured XML or JSON formats containing validated cryptographic QR codes.

What are the corporate income tax rates for Malaysian SMEs?

Eligible resident SMEs with paid-up capital of RM2.5 million or less enjoy preferential corporate tax rates: 15% on the first RM150,000 of chargeable income, 17% on income between RM150,001 and RM600,000, and standard 24% on remaining profits.

What licenses are required to operate a physical office or retail outlet in Kuala Lumpur?

Operating a commercial premise in Kuala Lumpur requires a Premise and Signboard License issued by Kuala Lumpur City Hall (Dewan Bandaraya Kuala Lumpur - DBKL), fire safety clearance from Bomba, and for foreign-owned retail/wholesale ventures, a WRT Approval from KPDN.
